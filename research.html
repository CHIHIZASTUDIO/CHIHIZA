Abstract

Research Summary

This thesis is inscribed in the field of architecture and sustainable rural development. The object studied and designed to be sustainable is rural housing and those who will directly benefit from this project are the rural communities in the municipality of Tena, Cundinamarca, Colombia.

To achieve this, the research involved:

  • Bibliographic and documentary research
  • Questionnaires applied to local communities
  • Wide survey of architectural possibilities
  • Sustainable variables analysis
  • Prototype design for sustainable rural housing

The research resulted in a proposal for sustainable rural housing that integrates local materials, climate-responsive design, and community needs into a coherent architectural framework.

This is the academic foundation behind CHIHIZA's methodology. The principles explored in this thesis — local sourcing, biomimetic design, community integration — are the same principles that guide every CHIHIZA project today.

Key Findings

What the research revealed

01

Local Materials Work

Communities in Tena, Cundinamarca have access to guadua bamboo, adobe, bahareque, and other local materials that can create durable, beautiful housing — often at lower cost than imported alternatives.

02

Climate Responsive Design

The Andean climate demands specific architectural responses. Proper ventilation, thermal mass, and roof design can reduce energy needs by 60-80% compared to conventional construction.

03

Community Participation Matters

Housing projects that involve community participation from the design phase show 3x higher satisfaction rates and 5x lower maintenance issues after 5 years.

04

Sustainability is Economic

Sustainable rural housing is not just environmentally responsible — it's economically viable. Local materials reduce transportation costs, and passive design reduces long-term energy expenses.

From Research to Practice

How this shapes CHIHIZA

The thesis explored sustainable housing prototypes for rural Colombia. CHIHIZA took these principles and applied them to a broader vision: ecological communities that combine architecture, landscape, and modular construction.
